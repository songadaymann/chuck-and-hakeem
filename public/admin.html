Pacing

Higher = the pile visibly moves more for the people who are here, and a quiet game is easier to win. Roughly: the men get in unless the average number of people online is about half this. It also caps how much one dig can do, so higher = each dig moves less. Digs are full strength until the pile is half this far ahead, then weaken, so a big crowd cannot finish weeks early. 1.28 makes the top machine about 9 times a shovel. 1 makes every tool equal.

Dirt bombs

The next lobby in line airdrops dirt back on, bringing the pile back to its schedule, at most 5% of it per bomb. Digging stops for 13 seconds. Keep "furthest ahead" above this, or the pile never gets far enough ahead to set one off. Each bomb gives a crowd that has caught up with its limit another 5% of the pile to dig at full speed, without moving the finish date. Shorter = more of the night spent visibly digging (and more bombs).

Crowd

Everyone past this digs a private practice pile until a spot opens. Raise it in steps while watching the numbers below. 0 closes the shared dig to newcomers. A dorm or an office shares one address. Lower it only if one address is hogging seats.

Spending guard

Cloudflare has no spending cap, so the game keeps its own estimate of today's bill (UTC day: it starts over at 7 pm Eastern). Past the budget nobody new gets into the shared dig (they dig a practice pile). Past twice the budget everyone is sent to practice until the day turns over. At the 4,000-player cap the game costs roughly $0.55 an hour.

Notice line

Before a deploy

A deploy restarts every room. Anything a player dug in the last minute and not yet written down would be lost. Press, wait about three seconds, then deploy.

Players

Counts every player code ever saved in this world (anyone who stayed long enough to be saved), and how many of them have dug at least 1 m3. Takes a few seconds.

Dirt bomb now

Drops one at once on that pile, the biggest a bomb may be (5% of it), whatever the schedule says. For testing, or for fun.
